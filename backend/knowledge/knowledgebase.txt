Introduction

Body weight is one of the most important indicators of overall health. Maintaining a healthy weight reduces the risk of diabetes, heart disease, stroke, hypertension, joint disorders, certain cancers, and many other chronic illnesses.

Weight management involves balancing calorie intake with calorie expenditure through healthy eating, regular physical activity, and sustainable lifestyle habits.

What is BMI?

Body Mass Index (BMI) is a screening tool that estimates whether a person's weight is appropriate for their height. It does not directly measure body fat but provides a useful indication of potential health risks associated with underweight, overweight, or obesity.

BMI Formula

BMI = Weight (kg) ÷ Height² (m²)

Example:

Height = 170 cm (1.70 m)

Weight = 70 kg

BMI = 70 ÷ (1.70 × 1.70)

BMI = 24.2

Q: Is BMI enough to determine health?

A: Exercise is important, but combining regular physical activity with a balanced diet and healthy lifestyle changes provides the best long-term results.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Can drinking too much water be harmful?

A: Yes. Excessive water intake over a short period can dilute sodium levels in the blood (hyponatraemia), which can be dangerous.

Q: Is thirst the best indicator of hydration?

A: Thirst is a useful signal, but urine colour, activity level, weather, and overall health should also be considered.

Q: Are tea and coffee counted as fluids?

A: Yes. They contribute to fluid intake, although water remains the preferred source for hydration.

Q: Does everyone need the same amount of water?

A: No. Fluid needs vary depending on age, body size, physical activity, climate, pregnancy, breastfeeding, and medical conditions.

SECTION 10
DIABETES & BLOOD SUGAR MANAGEMENT
=========================================================

Definition
----------
Diabetes mellitus is a chronic metabolic condition in which the body cannot effectively regulate blood glucose (blood sugar) levels. This may occur because the body produces too little insulin, does not use insulin effectively, or both.

Insulin is a hormone produced by the pancreas that helps glucose enter cells to be used for energy.

Persistent high blood sugar can damage the heart, kidneys, eyes, nerves, and blood vessels over time.

---------------------------------------------------------

Types of Diabetes
-----------------

Type 1 Diabetes

Definition

An autoimmune condition in which the immune system attacks the insulin-producing cells of the pancreas.

Characteristics

• Usually develops in childhood or young adulthood
• Requires lifelong insulin therapy
• Cannot currently be prevented

---------------------------------------------------------

Type 2 Diabetes

Definition

A condition where the body becomes resistant to insulin or does not produce enough insulin.

Characteristics

• Most common type of diabetes
• Usually develops in adults but can occur at any age
• Strongly associated with overweight, obesity, family history, and physical inactivity
• May be managed with lifestyle changes, medication, and sometimes insulin

---------------------------------------------------------

Gestational Diabetes

Definition

Diabetes that develops during pregnancy.

Importance

• Usually resolves after delivery
• Increases the future risk of Type 2 diabetes for both mother and child
• Requires monitoring during pregnancy

---------------------------------------------------------

Prediabetes
-----------

Definition

Prediabetes is a condition where blood glucose levels are higher than normal but not high enough for a diagnosis of diabetes.

Importance

Without lifestyle changes, prediabetes may progress to Type 2 diabetes.

SECTION 11
HYPERTENSION (HIGH BLOOD PRESSURE)
=========================================================

Definition
----------
Hypertension, also known as high blood pressure, is a long-term condition in which the force of blood against the walls of the arteries remains consistently higher than normal.

Over time, uncontrolled hypertension can damage the heart, brain, kidneys, eyes, and blood vessels.

Blood Pressure Basics
---------------------

Blood pressure is measured using two values:

Systolic Pressure

The pressure in the arteries when the heart contracts and pumps blood.

Diastolic Pressure

The pressure in the arteries when the heart relaxes between beats.

Example

120/80 mmHg

120 = Systolic Pressure

80 = Diastolic Pressure

---------------------------------------------------------

Blood Pressure Categories
-------------------------

Normal

Systolic: Less than 120 mmHg

AND

Diastolic: Less than 80 mmHg

---------------------------------------------------------

Elevated

Systolic: 120–129 mmHg

AND

Diastolic: Less than 80 mmHg

---------------------------------------------------------

Stage 1 Hypertension

Systolic: 130–139 mmHg

OR

Diastolic: 80–89 mmHg

---------------------------------------------------------

Stage 2 Hypertension

Systolic: 140 mmHg or higher

OR

Diastolic: 90 mmHg or higher

---------------------------------------------------------

Hypertensive Crisis

Systolic: 180 mmHg or higher

OR

Diastolic: 120 mmHg or higher

Requires immediate medical evaluation.

---------------------------------------------------------

Types of Hypertension
---------------------

Primary (Essential) Hypertension

• Most common type
• Develops gradually over many years
• No single identifiable cause

Secondary Hypertension

Occurs due to another medical condition such as:

• Kidney disease
• Hormonal disorders
• Certain medications
• Sleep apnoea

---------------------------------------------------------

Risk Factors
------------

Non-Modifiable

• Increasing age
• Family history
• Genetic factors

Modifiable

• Smoking
• Excess alcohol consumption
• Chronic stress
• Poor-quality diet
• Diabetes
• High cholesterol
• Inadequate sleep

-----------------

Many people with hypertension have no symptoms.

When symptoms occur, they may include:

• Headache
• Dizziness
• Blurred vision
• Fatigue
• Nosebleeds
• Shortness of breath
• Chest discomfort

These symptoms are not specific to hypertension and should be medically evaluated.

Diagnosis
---------

Hypertension is diagnosed using repeated blood pressure measurements over time.

A healthcare professional may recommend:

---------------------------------------------------------

Home Blood Pressure Monitoring

SECTION 12
HEART DISEASE & CARDIOVASCULAR HEALTH
=========================================================

Definition
----------
Cardiovascular disease (CVD) refers to a group of conditions affecting the heart and blood vessels. These conditions can reduce blood flow to vital organs and increase the risk of heart attack, stroke, heart failure, and other serious complications.

The cardiovascular system consists of:

• Heart
• Arteries
• Veins
• Capillaries

Its primary function is to deliver oxygen and nutrients throughout the body.

---------------------------------------------------------

Major Types of Heart Disease
----------------------------

Coronary Artery Disease (CAD)

Definition

Coronary artery disease occurs when the arteries supplying blood to the heart become narrowed or blocked due to the buildup of fatty deposits (atherosclerosis).

Possible Symptoms

• Chest pain (angina)
• Shortness of breath
• Fatigue
• Pain in the jaw, neck, shoulder, or arm
• Reduced exercise tolerance

---------------------------------------------------------

Heart Attack (Myocardial Infarction)

Definition

A heart attack occurs when blood flow to part of the heart muscle is suddenly blocked, causing damage to the heart tissue.

Common Warning Signs

• Chest pressure, tightness, or pain
• Pain spreading to the left arm, shoulder, jaw, neck, or back
• Shortness of breath
• Cold sweat
• Nausea or vomiting
• Dizziness
• Sudden weakness

Women, older adults, and people with diabetes may experience less typical symptoms such as unusual fatigue, nausea, or shortness of breath.

---------------------------------------------------------

Heart Failure

Definition

Heart failure occurs when the heart cannot pump blood efficiently enough to meet the body's needs.

Common Symptoms

• Shortness of breath
• Swelling of legs or ankles

SECTION 13
STROKE & BRAIN HEALTH
=========================================================

Definition
----------
A stroke occurs when the blood supply to part of the brain is interrupted or reduced, preventing brain tissue from receiving oxygen and nutrients. Brain cells begin to die within minutes without adequate blood flow.

Stroke is a medical emergency. Early recognition and prompt treatment can improve outcomes.

---------------------------------------------------------

How the Brain Works
-------------------

The brain controls:

• Movement
• Speech and language
• Memory
• Thinking and reasoning
• Vision
• Hearing
• Emotions
• Balance
• Coordination
• Breathing and other vital body functions

Healthy blood flow is essential for normal brain function.

---------------------------------------------------------

Types of Stroke
---------------

1. Ischaemic Stroke

Definition

Occurs when a blood clot blocks an artery supplying the brain.

Characteristics

• Most common type of stroke
• Often associated with atherosclerosis
• Can result from blood clots travelling from the heart or other arteries

---------------------------------------------------------

2. Haemorrhagic Stroke

Definition

Occurs when a weakened blood vessel in the brain ruptures, causing bleeding into or around the brain.

Possible Causes

• Uncontrolled high blood pressure
• Aneurysm
• Head injury
• Blood vessel abnormalities

---------------------------------------------------------

3. Transient Ischaemic Attack (TIA)

Definition

A temporary interruption of blood flow to the brain.

Characteristics

• Symptoms usually resolve within minutes or hours
• Does not cause permanent brain injury
• Is an important warning sign that a future stroke may occur

A TIA requires urgent medical evaluation.

---------------------------------------------------------

FAST Recognition Method
-----------------------

FAST helps identify common stroke symptoms.

F – Face

• One side of the face droops
• Uneven smile

A – Arms

• One arm becomes weak or numb
• Difficulty raising both arms equally

S – Speech

• Slurred speech
• Difficulty speaking
• Difficulty understanding speech

T – Time

If any of these symptoms occur, call emergency medical services immediately.

Do not wait for symptoms to improve.

---------------------------------------------------------

Other Possible Stroke Symptoms
------------------------------

• Sudden numbness or weakness of the face, arm, or leg
• Sudden confusion
• Sudden difficulty speaking
• Sudden loss of vision in one or both eyes
• Sudden dizziness
• Loss of balance
• Difficulty walking
• Severe sudden headache without a known cause

---------------------------------------------------------

Risk Factors
------------

Non-Modifiable

• Increasing age
• Family history
• Previous stroke or TIA
• Certain inherited conditions

Modifiable

• High blood pressure
• Diabetes
• High cholesterol
• Smoking
• Obesity
• Physical inactivity
• Excess alcohol
• Poor diet
• Heart disease
• Atrial fibrillation

---------------------------------------------------------

Possible Complications
----------------------

Depending on the area of the brain affected, complications may include:

• Weakness or paralysis
• Difficulty speaking
• Difficulty swallowing
• Memory problems
• Reduced concentration
• Vision problems
• Balance problems
• Emotional changes
• Depression
• Long-term disability

---------------------------------------------------------

Diagnosis
---------

Healthcare professionals may use:

• Neurological examination
• Blood tests
• CT scan
• MRI scan
• Carotid ultrasound
• Electrocardiogram (ECG)
• Echocardiogram
• CT or MR angiography

---------------------------------------------------------

Treatment Overview
------------------

Treatment depends on the type of stroke.

Possible treatments include:

• Medicines to dissolve or prevent blood clots (selected patients)
• Blood pressure management
• Surgery or procedures for some haemorrhagic strokes
• Rehabilitation therapies

Only qualified healthcare professionals can determine the appropriate treatment.

---------------------------------------------------------

---------------------

Recovery may involve:

• Physiotherapy
• Occupational therapy
• Speech and language therapy
• Nutritional support
• Psychological support
• Regular medical follow-up

Recovery varies from person to person.

• Attend regular health check-ups.

• Regular physical activity

• Puzzles and memory games
• Social interaction
• Staying mentally active

Healthy memory is supported by:

• Good sleep

• Managing blood pressure
• Managing diabetes

• Staying socially engaged

Seek medical evaluation if memory problems are persistent or worsening.

Lifestyle Recommendations

Healthy Eating

• Fruits

Avoid

• Smoking
• Excess alcohol

SECTION 14
CHOLESTEROL & LIPID DISORDERS
=========================================================

Definition
----------
Cholesterol is a waxy, fat-like substance found in every cell of the body. It is essential for producing hormones, vitamin D, and substances that help digest food.

Although cholesterol is necessary for normal body function, excessive cholesterol—particularly low-density lipoprotein (LDL) cholesterol—can accumulate in blood vessels and increase the risk of cardiovascular disease.

---------------------------------------------------------

What are Lipids?
----------------

Lipids are fats present in the blood. A standard lipid profile generally includes:

• Total Cholesterol
• LDL Cholesterol
• HDL Cholesterol
• Triglycerides
• Sometimes Very Low-Density Lipoprotein (VLDL)

---------------------------------------------------------

Types of Cholesterol
--------------------

Low-Density Lipoprotein (LDL)

Commonly called "Bad Cholesterol."

High LDL contributes to plaque buildup inside arteries.

High LDL increases the risk of:

• Heart attack
• Stroke
• Peripheral artery disease

---------------------------------------------------------

High-Density Lipoprotein (HDL)

Commonly called "Good Cholesterol."

HDL helps transport excess cholesterol from the bloodstream to the liver, where it can be removed from the body.

Higher HDL levels are generally associated with lower cardiovascular risk.

---------------------------------------------------------

Very Low-Density Lipoprotein (VLDL)

VLDL mainly carries triglycerides.

High VLDL levels may contribute to plaque formation and increase cardiovascular risk.

---------------------------------------------------------

Triglycerides
-------------

Triglycerides are a type of fat stored in the body and used for energy.

Persistently elevated triglycerides may increase the risk of cardiovascular disease and, at very high levels, pancreatitis.

---------------------------------------------------------

Healthy Lipid Profile Values
----------------------------

Total Cholesterol

Desirable

Less than 200 mg/dL

Borderline High

200–239 mg/dL

High

240 mg/dL or higher

---------------------------------------------------------

LDL Cholesterol

Optimal

Less than 100 mg/dL

Near Optimal

100–129 mg/dL

Borderline High

130–159 mg/dL

High

160–189 mg/dL

Very High

190 mg/dL or higher

---------------------------------------------------------

HDL Cholesterol

Men

40 mg/dL or higher

Women

50 mg/dL or higher

Higher HDL is generally protective.

---------------------------------------------------------

Triglycerides

Normal

Less than 150 mg/dL

Borderline High

150–199 mg/dL

High

200–499 mg/dL

Very High

500 mg/dL or higher

---------------------------------------------------------

Causes of High Cholesterol
--------------------------

Lifestyle Factors

• Diet high in saturated fat
• Diet high in trans fat
• Physical inactivity
• Smoking
• Excess alcohol
• Obesity

Medical Conditions

• Diabetes
• Hypothyroidism
• Kidney disease
• Liver disease

Other Factors

• Family history
• Increasing age
• Certain medications

---------------------------------------------------------

Familial Hypercholesterolaemia
------------------------------

Familial hypercholesterolaemia is an inherited condition that causes very high LDL cholesterol from an early age.

People with this condition have a much higher lifetime risk of premature cardiovascular disease and usually require specialist medical management.

---------------------------------------------------------

Possible Symptoms
-----------------

High cholesterol usually causes no symptoms.

Many people discover it only through a blood test.

Rarely, inherited disorders may cause:

• Yellow deposits around the eyes (xanthelasma)
• Tendon xanthomas (fatty deposits over tendons)

---------------------------------------------------------

Complications
-------------

Untreated high cholesterol may contribute to:

• Coronary artery disease
• Heart attack
• Stroke
• Peripheral artery disease
• Atherosclerosis

---------------------------------------------------------

Diagnosis
---------

Healthcare professionals may recommend:

• Lipid profile blood test
• Blood pressure measurement
• Blood glucose testing
• Cardiovascular risk assessment

Regular screening is particularly important for individuals with diabetes, hypertension, obesity, or a family history of cardiovascular disease.

Medication should only be started, adjusted, or stopped under the guidance of a qualified healthcare professional.

Frequently Asked Questions
--------------------------

Q: Can high cholesterol cause symptoms?

A: Usually no. Most people have no symptoms until complications develop, which is why regular screening is important.

Q: Can cholesterol be controlled without medication?

A: Some people can improve cholesterol levels through healthy eating, physical activity, weight management, and avoiding tobacco. Others may also require medication depending on their overall cardiovascular risk.

Q: Are all fats unhealthy?

A: No. Unsaturated fats found in foods such as olive oil, nuts, seeds, and fish can be part of a heart-healthy diet. Saturated and trans fats should be limited.

Q: How often should cholesterol be checked?

A: The appropriate screening interval depends on age, risk factors, and medical history. A healthcare professional can recommend a suitable schedule.

SECTION 15
OBESITY, METABOLIC SYNDROME & WEIGHT-RELATED DISORDERS
=========================================================

Definition
----------
Obesity is a chronic medical condition characterised by excessive body fat that may increase the risk of numerous health problems. It is commonly assessed using Body Mass Index (BMI), although waist circumference and overall body composition also provide important information.

---------------------------------------------------------

Body Mass Index (BMI)
---------------------

Formula

BMI = Weight (kg) ÷ Height² (m²)

BMI Categories

Underweight

Less than 18.5

Healthy Weight

18.5–24.9

Overweight

25.0–29.9

Obesity Class I

30.0–34.9

Obesity Class II

35.0–39.9

Obesity Class III (Severe Obesity)

40.0 or higher

BMI is a screening tool and should be interpreted alongside other health measures.

---------------------------------------------------------

Abdominal (Central) Obesity
---------------------------

Excess fat around the abdomen is strongly associated with:

• Type 2 diabetes
• Heart disease
• Stroke
• Metabolic syndrome

Waist Circumference

Higher Risk

Men

More than 102 cm

Women

More than 88 cm

Cut-off values may vary among different ethnic populations.

---------------------------------------------------------

What is Metabolic Syndrome?
---------------------------

Metabolic syndrome is a group of conditions that occur together and increase the risk of heart disease, stroke, and Type 2 diabetes.

These commonly include:

• Increased waist circumference
• Elevated blood pressure
• Elevated blood glucose
• High triglycerides
• Low HDL cholesterol

---------------------------------------------------------

Insulin Resistance
------------------

Definition

Insulin resistance occurs when body cells do not respond effectively to insulin, making it harder for glucose to enter cells.

Possible Consequences

• Prediabetes
• Type 2 diabetes
• Fatty liver disease
• Weight gain
• Cardiovascular disease

---------------------------------------------------------

Common Causes of Obesity
------------------------

Lifestyle Factors

• Excess calorie intake
• Large portion sizes
• Frequent consumption of sugary drinks
• Processed foods
• Physical inactivity
• Poor sleep

SECTION 16
KIDNEY HEALTH & CHRONIC KIDNEY DISEASE (CKD)
=========================================================

Definition
----------

The kidneys are two bean-shaped organs located on either side of the spine below the rib cage. They continuously filter blood to remove waste products, excess water, and toxins while maintaining the body's fluid, electrolyte, and acid-base balance.

Healthy kidneys are essential for maintaining overall health.

---------------------------------------------------------

Functions of the Kidneys
------------------------

The kidneys perform several important functions:

• Remove waste products from the blood
• Produce urine
• Maintain water balance
• Regulate blood pressure
• Balance electrolytes
• Maintain acid-base balance
• Activate Vitamin D
• Produce erythropoietin (a hormone that stimulates red blood cell production)

---------------------------------------------------------

Common Kidney Diseases
----------------------

1. Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)

Definition

A sudden decline in kidney function that develops over hours to days.

Possible Causes

• Severe dehydration
• Major infection (sepsis)
• Blood loss
• Certain medications
• Kidney obstruction

AKI may improve with prompt treatment depending on the cause.

---------------------------------------------------------

2. Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)

Definition

Chronic Kidney Disease is a long-term reduction in kidney function lasting three months or longer.

CKD often develops gradually and may not cause symptoms in its early stages.

Common Causes

• Diabetes
• High blood pressure
• Glomerular diseases
• Inherited kidney disorders
• Long-term kidney infections

---------------------------------------------------------

3. Kidney Stones

Definition

Kidney stones are hard mineral deposits that form inside the kidneys.

Possible Symptoms

• Severe pain in the back or side
• Blood in urine
• Pain during urination
• Nausea
• Vomiting
• Frequent urination

---------------------------------------------------------

Risk Factors
------------

• Diabetes
• High blood pressure
• Family history of kidney disease
• Smoking
• Obesity
• Older age
• Recurrent kidney infections
• Long-term use of certain pain medicines
• Kidney stones

---------------------------------------------------------

Possible Symptoms of Kidney Disease
----------------------------------

Early CKD often causes no symptoms.

As kidney function declines, symptoms may include:

• Fatigue
• Swelling of the feet, ankles, or hands
• Puffiness around the eyes
• Frequent urination, especially at night
• Reduced urine output
• Blood in urine
• Foamy urine
• Poor appetite
• Nausea
• Muscle cramps
• Difficulty concentrating
• Itchy skin

---------------------------------------------------------

Complications
-------------

Untreated kidney disease may lead to:

• High blood pressure
• Anaemia
• Bone disease
• Electrolyte imbalance
• Heart disease
• Fluid overload
• Kidney failure

---------------------------------------------------------

Important Kidney Tests
----------------------

Serum Creatinine

Creatinine is a waste product produced by muscles.

Higher creatinine levels may indicate reduced kidney function.

Interpretation should consider age, sex, muscle mass, and other clinical factors.

---------------------------------------------------------

Estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (eGFR)

eGFR estimates how well the kidneys filter blood.

General Interpretation

90 or above

Normal or high kidney function (if no other evidence of kidney disease)

60–89

Mildly reduced kidney function

30–59

Moderately reduced kidney function

15–29

Severely reduced kidney function

Below 15

Kidney failure

eGFR should always be interpreted together with medical history and other investigations.

---------------------------------------------------------

Urine Albumin / Protein

Healthy kidneys normally prevent significant protein loss into the urine.

Persistent protein (albumin) in urine may indicate kidney damage.

---------------------------------------------------------

Blood Urea Nitrogen (BUN)

BUN measures another waste product removed by the kidneys.

Elevated levels may occur due to:

• Kidney disease
• Dehydration
• High protein intake
• Other medical conditions

---------------------------------------------------------

Diagnosis
---------

Healthcare professionals may recommend:

• Blood tests
• Urine tests
• eGFR
• Creatinine
• Urine Albumin-Creatinine Ratio (ACR)
• Kidney ultrasound
• CT scan (selected cases)
• Kidney biopsy (selected cases)

---------------------------------------------------------

Stages of Chronic Kidney Disease
--------------------------------

Stage 1

Kidney damage with normal or near-normal kidney function.

Stage 2

Mild reduction in kidney function.

Stage 3

Moderate reduction in kidney function.

Stage 4

Severe reduction in kidney function.

Stage 5

Kidney failure.

---------------------------------------------------------

Healthy Kidney Diet
-------------------

Recommended Foods

• Fresh vegetables
• Fruits (according to medical advice)
• Whole grains
• Beans and lentils (when appropriate)
• Lean protein
• Fish
• Healthy oils

Limit

• Excess salt
• Processed foods
• Sugary drinks
• Excess alcohol

People with advanced kidney disease may require personalised advice regarding potassium, phosphorus, protein, and fluid intake.

---------------------------------------------------------

Hydration
----------

Adequate hydration supports normal kidney function.

However, people with advanced kidney disease or heart failure may need personalised fluid recommendations.

---------------------------------------------------------

Lifestyle Recommendations
-------------------------

• Control blood pressure.
• Manage diabetes.
• Maintain a healthy weight.
• Exercise regularly.
• Avoid smoking.
• Limit alcohol.
• Stay hydrated unless medically advised otherwise.
• Avoid unnecessary use of certain pain medicines (such as long-term NSAID use) unless prescribed.

---------------------------------------------------------

Dialysis
--------

Dialysis is a treatment that performs some of the filtering functions of the kidneys when kidney failure occurs.

Main Types

• Haemodialysis
• Peritoneal dialysis

Dialysis is recommended only after assessment by kidney specialists.

---------------------------------------------------------

Kidney Transplant
-----------------

A kidney transplant replaces a failed kidney with a healthy donor kidney.

Suitable candidates require detailed medical evaluation.

---------------------------------------------------------

Relationship with Other Diseases
--------------------------------

Kidney disease is closely linked with:

• Diabetes
• Hypertension
• Heart disease
• Obesity
• High cholesterol

Managing these conditions can help reduce kidney complications.

Frequently Asked Questions
--------------------------

Q: Can Chronic Kidney Disease be cured?

A: Chronic Kidney Disease usually cannot be reversed, but progression can often be slowed through good control of blood pressure, diabetes, healthy lifestyle choices, and appropriate medical treatment.

Q: Does drinking more water always improve kidney health?

A: Adequate hydration is important, but excessive fluid intake is not beneficial for everyone. People with advanced kidney disease or heart failure may require personalised fluid advice.

Q: Can diabetes damage the kidneys?

A: Yes. Diabetes is one of the leading causes of Chronic Kidney Disease. Good blood sugar control can help reduce the risk of kidney complications.

Q: Can kidney disease occur without symptoms?

A: Yes. Early kidney disease often causes no symptoms, making regular health check-ups and laboratory testing important for people at increased risk.

SECTION 17
LIVER HEALTH & LIVER DISEASES
=========================================================

Definition
----------

The liver is the largest internal organ in the human body. It performs hundreds of essential functions, including processing nutrients, producing proteins, storing energy, filtering harmful substances, and supporting digestion.

A healthy liver is vital for maintaining metabolism, immunity, and overall well-being.

---------------------------------------------------------

Functions of the Liver
----------------------

The liver performs many important functions:

• Produces bile to help digest fats
• Stores vitamins and minerals
• Stores glycogen for energy
• Removes toxins from the blood
• Processes medications
• Produces proteins involved in blood clotting
• Helps regulate blood sugar
• Supports immune function
• Breaks down old red blood cells

---------------------------------------------------------

Common Liver Diseases
---------------------

1. Metabolic Dysfunction-Associated Steatotic Liver Disease (MASLD)

Previously known as Non-Alcoholic Fatty Liver Disease (NAFLD).

Definition

MASLD occurs when excess fat accumulates in the liver in people with metabolic risk factors such as obesity, diabetes, or dyslipidaemia, without another primary cause of liver disease.

Risk Factors

• Obesity
• Type 2 diabetes
• High cholesterol
• High triglycerides
• Metabolic syndrome
• Physical inactivity

Possible Symptoms

Many people have no symptoms.

Some may experience:

• Fatigue
• Mild discomfort in the upper right abdomen
• General weakness

---------------------------------------------------------

2. Alcohol-Related Liver Disease

Definition

Excessive alcohol consumption can damage liver cells over time.

Stages may include:

• Fatty liver
• Alcoholic hepatitis
• Cirrhosis

Reducing or stopping alcohol consumption can improve outcomes in many people.

---------------------------------------------------------

3. Viral Hepatitis
------------------

Hepatitis A

Usually spread through contaminated food or water.

Often causes an acute illness and many people recover completely.

---------------------------------------------------------

Hepatitis B

Spread through infected blood and certain body fluids.

Some people develop chronic infection.

Vaccination can help prevent Hepatitis B.

---------------------------------------------------------

Hepatitis C

Primarily spread through infected blood.

Chronic infection may occur.

Effective treatments are available for many people.

---------------------------------------------------------

Hepatitis D

Occurs only in people infected with Hepatitis B.

---------------------------------------------------------

Hepatitis E

Usually spread through contaminated water.

Often causes an acute illness.

---------------------------------------------------------

4. Cirrhosis

Definition

Cirrhosis is advanced scarring of the liver resulting from long-term liver injury.

Common Causes

• Chronic viral hepatitis
• Alcohol-related liver disease
• MASLD
• Autoimmune liver disease
• Certain inherited disorders

Possible Symptoms

• Fatigue
• Swelling of legs
• Abdominal swelling (ascites)
• Easy bruising
• Yellowing of the skin and eyes (jaundice)
• Confusion in advanced disease

---------------------------------------------------------

5. Liver Failure

Definition

Liver failure occurs when the liver can no longer perform its essential functions adequately.

This may develop suddenly (acute) or gradually (chronic).

It requires urgent medical care.

---------------------------------------------------------

Jaundice
--------

Definition

Jaundice is yellow discoloration of the skin and eyes caused by increased bilirubin levels.

Possible Causes

• Liver disease
• Gallstone obstruction
• Viral hepatitis
• Certain blood disorders

---------------------------------------------------------

Risk Factors
------------

• Obesity
• Diabetes
• High cholesterol
• Excess alcohol
• Viral hepatitis
• Certain medications
• Family history of liver disease
• Exposure to toxins

---------------------------------------------------------

Possible Symptoms
-----------------

Early liver disease may have no symptoms.

As disease progresses, symptoms may include:

• Fatigue
• Loss of appetite
• Nausea
• Weight loss
• Right upper abdominal discomfort
• Dark urine
• Pale stools
• Itching
• Swelling of legs
• Abdominal swelling
• Jaundice

---------------------------------------------------------

Complications
-------------

Untreated liver disease may lead to:

• Cirrhosis
• Liver failure
• Portal hypertension
• Internal bleeding
• Ascites
• Hepatic encephalopathy
• Liver cancer

---------------------------------------------------------

Liver Function Tests (LFTs)
---------------------------

Alanine Aminotransferase (ALT)

ALT is an enzyme primarily found in liver cells.

Elevated ALT may suggest liver cell injury.

---------------------------------------------------------

AST is present in the liver and other tissues.

Elevated AST may indicate liver damage but is not specific to the liver.

---------------------------------------------------------

ALP may increase in conditions affecting the bile ducts or liver.

---------------------------------------------------------

Bilirubin

Bilirubin is produced during the breakdown of red blood cells.

High bilirubin levels may cause jaundice.

---------------------------------------------------------

Albumin

Albumin is a protein produced by the liver.

Low albumin may occur in advanced liver disease and other medical conditions.

• Whole grains

• Fish

• Nuts
• Healthy oils

Limit

• Sugary beverages
• Highly processed foods
• Excess saturated fat
• Excess alcohol

-------------------------

--------------------------------

• Cardiovascular disease

Q: Can fatty liver disease be reversed?

Q: Does liver disease always cause symptoms?

Q: Can liver function tests diagnose every liver disease?

Q: Can asthma be cured?

Q: Does quitting smoking improve lung health?

A: Yes. Stopping smoking reduces the risk of COPD, lung cancer, cardiovascular disease, and other smoking-related illnesses. Lung function decline also slows after smoking cessation.

Q: Are probiotics necessary for everyone?

Q: When should blood in the stool be evaluated?

A: Any unexplained blood in the stool should be assessed by a healthcare professional, especially if it is persistent or accompanied by abdominal pain, weight loss, dizziness, or fatigue.

SECTION 20
BONE, JOINT & MUSCULOSKELETAL HEALTH
=========================================================

Definition
----------

The musculoskeletal system consists of bones, joints, muscles, tendons, ligaments, and cartilage. Together, these structures provide support, enable movement, protect internal organs, and maintain posture.

Healthy bones and muscles are essential for daily activities, physical independence, and overall quality of life.

---------------------------------------------------------

Components of the Musculoskeletal System
----------------------------------------

Bones

• Provide body structure
• Protect organs
• Store minerals such as calcium and phosphorus
• Produce blood cells in bone marrow

Joints

• Connect bones
• Allow movement
• Absorb mechanical stress

Muscles

• Produce movement
• Maintain posture
• Generate body heat

Ligaments

• Connect bone to bone
• Stabilise joints

Tendons

• Connect muscle to bone
• Transfer force to produce movement

Cartilage

• Covers bone ends within joints
• Reduces friction
• Cushions joints during movement

---------------------------------------------------------

Common Musculoskeletal Disorders
--------------------------------

1. Osteoarthritis

Definition

Osteoarthritis is a degenerative joint disease caused by gradual breakdown of joint cartilage.

Commonly Affected Joints

• Knees
• Hips
• Hands

• Joint pain
• Morning stiffness lasting less than 30 minutes
• Reduced flexibility
• Swelling
• Grinding sensation during movement

Definition

Rheumatoid arthritis is a chronic autoimmune disease that causes inflammation of multiple joints.

Possible Symptoms

• Joint pain
• Swelling
• Warm joints
• Morning stiffness lasting longer than one hour
• Fatigue
• Reduced joint movement

Early diagnosis and treatment can help reduce joint damage.

3. Osteoporosis

Definition

Osteoporosis is a condition in which bones become weaker and more likely to fracture.

Risk Factors

• Increasing age
• Female sex after menopause
• Low calcium intake
• Vitamin D deficiency
• Smoking
• Physical inactivity
• Long-term corticosteroid use

Frequently Asked Questions
--------------------------

Q: Can exercise worsen arthritis?

A: Appropriate, low-impact exercise is beneficial for many people with arthritis. It can improve joint flexibility, muscle strength, and overall function. Exercise plans should be individualised.

Q: How can osteoporosis be prevented?

A: Prevention includes adequate calcium and vitamin D intake, regular weight-bearing exercise, avoiding smoking, limiting alcohol, and reducing fall risk.

Q: Is back pain always caused by a slipped disc?

A: No. Back pain has many possible causes, including muscle strain, poor posture, arthritis, osteoporosis, and other spinal conditions. A healthcare professional can help determine the underlying cause.

Q: When should joint pain be medically evaluated?

A: Joint pain that is severe, persistent, associated with swelling, redness, fever, significant stiffness, or reduced function should be assessed by a healthcare professional.
